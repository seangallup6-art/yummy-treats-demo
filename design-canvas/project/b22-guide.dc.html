Switching your dog to a new food without an upset stomach

Reviewed by [VET NAME], small-animal vet, Bangkok4 min readUpdated [DATE]
[PHOTO: two bowls side by side, old food and new food, a dog looking at both]

Dogs do not get bored of food the way we do, but their gut does get used to it. Change everything in one meal and you often get loose stools for a few days. Change it over a week and most dogs never notice. Here is the plan we give every new customer.

The seven-day mix

Keep the total amount per day the same as before. Only the proportions change. Use the feeding table on the new bag for the total.

DaysOld foodNew foodWatch for
1 and 23/41/4Normal stools, normal appetite
3 and 41/21/2Slightly softer stools are fine
5 and 61/43/4Drinking as usual
7 onwardsnoneallCoat and energy over the next month

If it goes wrong

Loose stools for a day: go back one step for two more days, then carry on. Refusing the bowl: warm water on the kibble brings out the smell; do not add toppings yet, or you will be adding them forever. Vomiting, blood, or no appetite for more than a day: stop and see your vet. This is not a food problem to push through.

Puppies, seniors and dogs with a sensitive stomach: take ten days instead of seven. Cats: the same idea, but slower still, because a cat that stops eating for two days is a vet visit.
